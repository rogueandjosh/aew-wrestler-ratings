About the Ratings

Wrestling is all about feelings, emotions and opinions. It will never be possible to completely accurately compare wrestlers' current form - there are so many factors to consider. However, this system takes account of multiple factors so that wrestlers can be directly compared and tracked over time. It equates them to a numerical value where '100' is average.

The ratings are based on match performances from the excellent Cagematch.net site - not wins and losses, but how fans have received and rated each AEW wrestler's match.

This is an independent fan project. AEW and Cagematch are the property of their respective owners.

Key Points:

  • All wrestlers start at 100 - points gained/lost based on Cagematch match ratings and votes
  • Factors include: current rating, opponent quality, show type, and match stakes
  • Five divisions: Men's, Women's, Men's Tag, Women's Tag, and Trios (crossover allowed)
  • Minimum 2 matches required to appear in rankings
  • Ratings degrade over time toward 100 - wrestlers drop out when they reach 100

📌 Important Context

This rating system does NOT account for intangibles like charisma or promo ability - it is purely based on match performance from Cagematch. It reflects both how well wrestlers have performed AND how well they are being booked (not for wins, but for opportunities to perform).

🎯 How Ratings Work

All wrestlers start at 100 (average). Points are gained and lost depending on Cagematch match ratings and votes, plus show ratings and votes.

⚙️ Factors Affecting Rating Changes

Multiple factors determine how much is gained or lost: the wrestler's rating before the match, opponent quality, the show type, match stakes, and other considerations.

🏆 The Five Divisions

Men's, Women's, Men's Tag, Women's Tag, and Trios. There is crossover - for example, a male wrestler in a trios team would contribute to both Men's ratings and potentially Men's Tag if part of a tag team within the trio.

✅ Entry Requirements

Two matches are needed to gain a ranking.

⏳ Time Degradation

Rating points gained degrade over time; rating points lost are gradually recovered. Eventually, after weeks, months, or possibly years, a wrestler/team's rating returns to 100. Once a rating reaches 100, the wrestler/team drops out of the rankings.

📅 Update Schedule

Rankings refresh each Wednesday before Dynamite.

📊 How It Works — Example

Two matches, both rated 9.08 on Cagematch with approximately 275 votes each. But the rating points gained were very different:

Swerve Strickland vs Kenny Omega → Swerve gained 6.54 points
Tommaso Ciampa vs Kyle Fletcher → Tommaso gained 11.63 points

Why did Tommaso gain almost double? Several factors were at play:

Tommaso's lower rating going in (109.06 vs Swerve's 124.14) — a lower-rated wrestler gains more from a strong performance, just as you'd expect in any ranking system. There's more room to climb.

A title was on the line — the TNT Championship has its own rating which boosts points gained, and a title change adds further.

The show rating was higher — the Dynamite episode featuring Tommaso's match was rated 9.11 overall, compared to 8.47 for Swerve's. A higher show rating amplifies the gains.

Swerve did have some advantages — Kenny Omega's rating (128.05) was lower than Kyle Fletcher's (149.46), meaning Swerve was facing a relatively lower-rated opponent. You'd expect a high-rated match against a higher-rated opponent to count for less — because that result is less surprising. Swerve also benefited from being in the main event, which carries a spotlight bonus.

The key takeaway: the same match quality score can produce very different rating changes depending on the wrestler's current rating, opponent quality, match stakes, card position, and show quality. If we swapped the opponents and moved Tommaso's match to the main event, he'd have gained even more and Swerve would have gained less.
